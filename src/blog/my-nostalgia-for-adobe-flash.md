Every 2000s and 2010s kid grew up playing Flash games on crappy school computers. I would often chill at the library
playing Bloons TD 5 on my Chromebook. It was fun (and still is) plopping down monkeys that throw darts at the evil
"bloons." I would get into some trouble of course, but hey, a rat is going to be a rat. Here's some of my most favorite
Flash games to name a few:

- Bloons TD 4 and Bloons TD 5
- Duck Life (All of them were good)
- Stick RPG 1 and 2
- Papa's Games (especially Wingeria and Freezeria)
- Boxhead The Zombie Wars
- Learn to Fly
- Super Smash Flash 2
- Poptropica
- Free Rider 2
- Ragdoll Cannon 3

Feel free to comment on what your favorite Flash games were, and I'll try to remember if I've played them.

Unfortunately, Flash was deprecated in 2017 and would meet its demise in late 2020. It was due to a combination of
factors, such as HTML5 making it obsolete, but most importantly, _screw you Steve Jobs you hypocrite 😡_.

Anyway, the real reason why I'm writing about this topic is because Ninja Kiwi, developer of Bloons TD 6 (aka _Funny
Monke Game_), announced today
[in a Reddit post](https://www.reddit.com/r/NinjaKiwiOfficial/comments/1xsncao/nk_archive_account_shutdown/) that they
will be shutting down the account service for their Flash games within the coming month. With the rise of AI-assisted
vulnerability attacks such as PHP2Shell, it was inevitable that Flash would become the next target. This issue doesn't
mean Ninja Kiwi's Flash games are fading away entirely; they will still be freely accessible through NK Archive on
Steam. However, you will lose access to logging in with your Ninja Kiwi account, in-game leaderboards, and syncing your
account progress. Before sailing off into the sunset, Ninja Kiwi will be giving everyone who already has an account
10,000 NK Coins to spend on in-game premium items. Have fun while you still can xd.

## Preservation Efforts

Credit to the team at [Ruffle](https://ruffle.rs/) for making it easier to emulate Flash in the browser. The cool thing
is that it can run either on the client or on the server, and it's completely free and open-source.

Credit to [Flashpoint Archive](https://flashpointarchive.org/) for compiling a massive database of Flash games,
animations, and everything else Flash-related. They even provide a launcher allowing you to easily play your favorite
Flash games.

Credit to [Newgrounds](https://www.newgrounds.com/) for effectively being a time capsule of all the nightmares I had in
my sleep.
